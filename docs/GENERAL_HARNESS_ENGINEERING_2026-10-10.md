# Harness engineering review for Clinical Orchestra 2

10 October 2026. This review supplements the [broad literature review](HARNESS_LITERATURE_REVIEW_2026-10-04.md), [science harness investigation](SCIENCE_HARNESS_INTELLIGENCE_2026-10-08.md), and [architecture](NEXT_STEP_ARCHITECTURE.md). It focuses on evidence about the harness itself: context, tools, control flow, execution, failure handling, and evaluation. It does not change the task from forecasting the next **documented** clinical event. D-21 records the design decision drawn from this review.

## What the recent evidence says

| Primary source | Result relevant to us | Limit of transfer |
| --- | --- | --- |
| [MedicalHarness (5 October 2026)](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.05778v1) | Five open-weight models, five harnesses, three seeds and 107 executable medical tasks. Model effects explained 58% of outcome variance; harness and model–harness interaction each explained 12%, with the remainder varying by run. On calculation and long-context retrieval, differences in answer production often exceeded differences on tasks all harnesses answered. Tool exposure, submission checks and context management had task- and model-dependent effects. | These are calculations, retrieval, workflows and browser tasks, not case-report forecasting. The variance shares and effect sizes cannot be imported into our benchmark. It is a new preprint. |
| [Apple/EPFL autonomous ML engineering comparison (30 September 2026)](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.40303v1) | Under matched backbone and time budget, a minimal coding agent matched or beat several elaborate ML-engineering harnesses on the studied tasks. Larger orchestration was not a dependable gain for strong backbones. | ML engineering has executable feedback and long sessions. A simple forecast call has different information needs; this result is a reason to test complexity, not proof that it never helps. |
| [Coding-harness component study (17 September 2026)](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.20804v1) | In 176 matched settings, context management mattered most when a trajectory would overflow. Staged elision reduced cost; recoverable elided content was rarely recalled and gave no measured accuracy gain. Planning and predefined tools helped differently across model capability and task type. | These are coding tasks. Our short forecasting runs may never trigger compaction; the action interface and context policy must be tested here before adoption. |
| [Model–harness fit study (1 October 2026)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.00917) | Across 66 model–harness configurations on three agent benchmarks, rankings changed with harness and task. Some harnesses were cheaper and better for particular models. | Coding/terminal tasks; it does not rank harnesses or models for clinical forecasts. |
| [Harness evolution re-evaluation (version 5 October 2026)](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.12227v4) and [tool-agent harness selection study (4 September 2026)](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.05736v1) | Harness search can look better than it is when search and reporting share tasks or when the comparison omits simple extra-inference controls. The latter study reports held-out mean lift, worst-condition behavior and reliability of the *selected* configuration. | Their optimizers and benchmarks are not our method. Their experimental controls are directly useful. Both are preprints. |
| [Anthropic infrastructure-noise experiment (5 February 2026)](https://www.anthropic.com/engineering/infrastructure-noise) | Resource headroom changed both error rates and eventual task success in terminal-agent evaluations. Limits, concurrency and service conditions can change the measured system. | The reported percentage-point differences are specific to coding tasks; do not apply them as correction factors to clinical results. |
| [Silent Failures in Agentic Security Evaluation (26 September 2026)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.32691) | The authors found a benchmark where payloads were sometimes not delivered and tool-identity scoring misread actual tool arguments. Re-scoring the same traces changed a reported 21.7% success rate to 1.2%. | It studies prompt-injection defenses, not diagnosis. The general lesson is to validate that a test stimulus reached the worker and that scoring reads the executed event, not the intended one. |
| [Anthropic agent evaluation guidance](https://www.anthropic.com/engineering/demystifying-evals-for-ai-agents), [tool-design guidance](https://www.anthropic.com/engineering/writing-tools-for-agents), and [Inspect log documentation](https://inspect.aisi.org.uk/eval-logs.html) | Separate task, trial, transcript and grader; test tool descriptions and outputs; retain provider request IDs and error calls. | These are practitioner guidance and framework documentation, not estimates of clinical benefit. Our local ledger and scorer remain authoritative until an adapter passes parity checks. |

The consistent conclusion is narrower than “agents help” or “agents do not help.” A reported score belongs to a **model, harness, task, budget and environment together**. We should keep a capable direct predictor as a real competitor and treat each additional mechanism as an experiment. A delivery or scoring failure can produce an impressive but false benchmark result even when the model is unchanged.

## Separate measurement infrastructure from forecast policy

Some harness work is necessary for a valid experiment even if it never improves accuracy: input isolation, frozen labels, a strict output contract, durable transport records, a deterministic scorer, a budget broker and replay. These are part of the measurement instrument. They must be the **same** across competing strategies.

Other work is a forecast intervention: an explicit plan, evidence retrieval, a critic, editable notes, programmatic tool use, summarization, delegation or model routing. Do not introduce one as a hidden default. Give it a versioned name, a declared information and budget contract, and a paired comparison against direct and repeated-request forecasts. A “reviewed” score is a mixed-model system score if review calls a different model.

The first run should be deliberately small. The direct policy sees the clinical prefix, typed target and candidate IDs and returns one probability vector. The same fixed worker boundary then supports repeated requests, a one-evidence-action control and, later, bounded programmatic execution. This tests what the extra machinery contributes while avoiding a framework migration before the metric is trusted. [Anthropic's original engineering guidance](https://www.anthropic.com/engineering/building-effective-agents) recommends starting with the simplest sufficient loop and inspecting framework abstractions that can hide prompts and responses; our local replay requirement makes that practical advice especially important.

## Operational contracts before any paid comparison

### Freeze the assigned task and actual context

Give the worker only an input-only task package: opaque item ID, schema version, family/subtype, cutoff, endpoint, visible facts with known-at status, frozen alternatives and OTHER. The source article, hidden physical chronology, target fact IDs, answer ID, construction audit and rationale remain outside the worker and its tools. A retrieval result is a new bounded evidence record; it must not smuggle the source ending into context. Save the **rendered message list actually sent** to the provider and its digest, because comparing prompt templates alone does not prove equal context. Include tool definitions and system/developer messages in that record, excluding secrets.

Treat absence of a future fact in the rendered prompt and tool output as a tested invariant. Use synthetic sentinel facts in hidden labels, source endings, opaque IDs and disallowed artifact namespaces. For each leakage control, first prove the sentinel is in the forbidden source location and that the attempted route was exercised; then assert it never appears in allowed messages or results. This is analogous to validating payload delivery in the [security-harness audit](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.32691). A test that never reaches the boundary cannot demonstrate protection.

### One attempt is an auditable state machine

For each assigned item and policy, create an attempt record before dispatch. Record transitions such as `assigned`, `request_prepared`, `dispatched`, `response_saved`, `parsed`, `submitted`, `scored`, or an explicit terminal failure. Save each provider response before parsing or a dependent call. A timeout after dispatch has an **unknown provider outcome**, not a free retry. Any retry gets its own ID, reason, policy limit and possible cost. An identical committed response can be replayed and rescored without asking the model again. Never overwrite the earlier forecast when an enhanced policy revises it.

An attempt needs stable links to: task/option/label bundle digests; source and split version; requested and actual model ID; provider and adapter version; rendered prompt and tool schema digests; temperature and effective generation settings; policy and broker versions; resource limits; scheduled trial/seed; provider request/response IDs where available; raw response; parsed vector or error; usage and cost provenance; elapsed time; every tool request **and executed arguments/result**; and scorer version. Unknown usage stays unknown. Raw responses may contain sensitive case material and belong in access-controlled run artifacts, not commit history. Inspect's [log schema and request-ID handling](https://inspect.aisi.org.uk/eval-logs.html) are useful adapter references, but our local append-only records remain sufficient without Inspect.

Preflight a model/provider with a small fixed contract probe before assigning scored cases: verify the actual model ID, API dialect, structured-output behavior, candidate-ID coverage, truncation signal, token accounting and temperature behavior. Once a run starts, provider failures remain in the assigned denominator. A live API model alias may change; store the provider-returned identity when available and treat an unreported revision as an explicit limitation. Do not silently fall back to another model.

Keep two comparison tracks distinct. The **common-protocol track** uses the same task wording, candidate ordering rule, output schema, information access and policy limits across models; provider adapters may translate transport and structured-output syntax but cannot add clinical hints. This supports a controlled model comparison while exposing interface-compatibility failures. A later **adapted-system track** may tune prompt or tool presentation for a model on development cases under the same declared search budget; report it as a model–harness pair and never mix its score into the common-protocol ranking. [Model–harness fit evidence](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.00917) makes both tracks useful and warns against calling either one the model's intrinsic capability.

### Errors, submission and limits are outcomes

Validate probabilities for finite values, exact candidate-ID coverage, uniqueness, bounds and sum tolerance. A schema repair can be a named policy with its own call and budget, but an implicit repair changes the harness. Define one final submission channel. If a model gives an answer in some other place, retain the raw evidence and mark the primary attempt `invalid_submission`; an optional exploratory parser can measure how much the format rule costs, without changing the frozen main score. The [MedicalHarness paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.05778v1) found that where the answer appeared changed rankings for its tasks, so this is a substantive protocol choice.

Use a failure taxonomy that distinguishes: unassigned/preflight failure, provider rejection, unknown transport outcome, timeout, budget exhaustion, context overflow, tool denial, malformed tool call, unavailable evidence, missing final submission, invalid vector, and scorer/integrity failure. The first group is a readiness problem; later failures after assignment are part of operational performance. A scorer or leakage-integrity failure stops the run rather than being counted as a wrong medical forecast.

Host limits must cover wall time, model calls, input/output tokens, tool actions, parallel branches, artifact size, retrieval volume and reviews. The same predeclared limit applies to nested operations; log observed usage even when it exceeds a reservation. Provider caches and case ordering affect price and latency, so preserve case-local ordering and log cache hits; do not present lower invoice cost alone as evidence of better reasoning. Compare both equal call/time limits and achieved cost/latency. [Infrastructure experiments](https://www.anthropic.com/engineering/infrastructure-noise) show why a resource setting belongs in the scientific protocol.

### Tools and context should earn their complexity

Start with a small typed tool surface only when a task has a real need for external evidence or computation. A tool description must state inputs, allowed scope, output shape, truncation, errors and whether the operation changes state. Return bounded excerpts with stable provenance IDs. Hide neither a failed tool call nor an empty result. A tool search layer is not automatically better than exposing a few relevant tools; [MedicalHarness](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.05778v1) found model-dependent regressions from tool discovery, and [Anthropic's tool-design guide](https://www.anthropic.com/engineering/writing-tools-for-agents) treats tool specifications as testable interfaces.

The direct and one-action forecast histories are short. Do not add model-written compaction or long-term memory until measured context overflow or task need justifies it. If a longer programmatic run requires trimming, first elide large stale tool outputs while preserving the input contract, current evidence IDs, final instructions and recent actions. Measure whether the model recalls externalized content before building a recall index. Never compact in a way that converts an unresolved hypothesis into a patient fact or lets one cutoff inherit another cutoff's notes. The [component ablation](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.20804v1) supports conditional, rather than universal, context machinery.

## How to decide whether an enhancement helped

1. **Separate sources of variation.** Split by case/article cluster before development. Choose policies and prompts only on development cases. Use untouched cases once for the confirmatory comparison. Give all policies the same frozen item IDs, labels, evidence access and runtime version. If a policy adds an evidence source, that is the named intervention. Interleave model–policy runs by case to reduce provider-time confounding; preserve within-case prefix order for caching and record the schedule.
2. **Compare against enough inference.** Direct single-call prediction is the basic reference. A fixed repeated-request vector average is the stronger control. An enhanced loop must beat both under a declared budget, not merely beat one sample while spending more calls. Track candidate changes from initial to final: wrong-to-right, right-to-wrong, and Brier/log-loss changes without a top-choice change.
3. **Report all assigned attempts.** Headline tables should give per-family completion, operational top-choice accuracy over all assigned items, valid-only Brier/log loss with denominator, failures by class, calls/tokens/latency/cost, and uncertainty clustered by case. Matched answered-item scores are diagnostic only because the subset can change with policy. Do not use an outcome on a small valid-only subset to rank a failure-prone strategy.
4. **Ablate one mechanism at a time.** When comparing tools, keep the worker, model, prompt style, budget and scorer constant. For compaction, verify it actually triggered. For a review step, verify it completed before submission. For retrieval, verify the intended corpus and exclusion filter were applied. Report the number of trials and policy variants explored, including discarded variants. A variant selected after many development trials needs a fresh case-level check.
5. **Promote only a reproducible trade-off.** Before a pilot, declare a primary family/metric and a practical cost or latency ceiling. On development data, retain an enhancement only if its paired case-level estimate is positive with uncertainty narrow enough to be useful, completion does not materially regress, and the added cost fits the declared ceiling. If evidence is inconclusive, keep the direct policy as the reference and label the enhancement exploratory. Do not set a numerical gain threshold from the test cases. Recheck on at least one other provider family before claiming the mechanism is general. The [harness-evolution re-evaluation](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.12227v4) and [selection study](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.05736v1) motivate these controls.

The benchmark's own label quality remains a separate uncertainty. Exact-ID scoring is repeatable but cannot make a poorly supported or hindsight-selected question valid. Keep construction rejection reasons, observation status, endpoint origin, OTHER rates and model-audit disagreement visible. No human review is available; therefore neither agreement among constructor models nor a positive harness ablation should be called clinical validation.

## Development order and executable acceptance gates

1. **Deterministic contracts and scorer.** Add input-only and hidden-output schemas, a manifest builder, probability validation, Brier/log loss, case-cluster aggregation and mechanical replay. Use synthetic hand-calculated examples across all five families. This needs no API keys. An item with future text in its public contract must fail before any call.
2. **Attempt journal and direct worker.** Add the state machine, raw response capture, provider-identity record, strict submission and failure taxonomy. Fake-provider tests cover success, truncation, timeout after dispatch, malformed vectors, duplicate calls and replay. An assigned failure cannot disappear from the denominator. Preflight tests must not alter a scored item.
3. **Broker and isolation.** Implement capabilities and nested budgets in the host, even for a worker with no tools. Fixture attacks try guessed label paths, cross-item evidence IDs, symlink traversal, unprinted tool results, nested model calls and concurrent budget oversubscription. Verify route exposure for each negative control. No gold-bearing object crosses the worker boundary.
4. **One real development pilot when credentials and cases are ready.** Compare the direct policy and repeated-request control on declared development cases, first checking export support and completion. Only then add one evidence action. Do not consume the 99 post-cutoff historical items as development examples.
5. **Optional agent policies.** Add bounded programmatic use, context management and review separately when a measured failure motivates each one. An Inspect adapter is acceptable only after its raw records and score agree with the framework-free core on identical saved attempts. Freeze the winning protocol before untouched-case evaluation.

The minimal module boundaries are `forecasting/contracts.py` for frozen task and label types, `evaluation/predictions.py` for submission parsing, `evaluation/scoring.py` and `evaluation/reporting.py` for deterministic metrics, `harness/worker.py` for input-only execution, `harness/broker.py` for capability and budget enforcement, and the existing ledger for append-only attempt records. A policy should consume an input-only task plus broker interface and return a versioned forecast reference; only the scorer can join that reference to a hidden label. These are planned interfaces, not current files. Keep optional framework adapters outside the scoring core.

These are software and experiment gates, not a claim that the benchmark is clinically valid or that any tool policy will win. The immediate coding starting point is still the deterministic contracts and scorer.
