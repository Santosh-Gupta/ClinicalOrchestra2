# Projects to consult as Clinical Orchestra develops

Checked 9 October 2026. This is a problem index for future implementation work, not a list of frameworks to install. Open the linked project when its issue arises; inspect the current paper, code and configuration before adopting a technique. Keep our task contract, information cutoff and hidden-label boundary intact. The [broad literature review](HARNESS_LITERATURE_REVIEW_2026-10-04.md), [science harness investigation](SCIENCE_HARNESS_INTELLIGENCE_2026-10-08.md), and [D-17 through D-20](DESIGN_DECISIONS.md) contain the earlier reasoning.

## Closest clinical forecasting references

| If we encounter… | Consult | Specific question to investigate | Transfer limit |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Trouble defining action windows, action bundles or missing versus extra actions | [ClinicBench](https://atlasdiscovery.bio/clinicbench) | Its August 2026 oncology benchmark forecasts tests, imaging, treatment and other actions over the next 30 days from a record cutoff. Examine its examples and error breakdown for how it treats planned versus performed actions, incomplete regimens, omitted actions and plausible extras. | It uses private longitudinal oncology records and a rubric judge for free-text plans. Its score does not validate our case-report labels or solve the noisy-judge problem. A public implementation was not identified from the project page. |
| Suspicion that our model comparison ignores simple clinical prediction baselines | [ClinicalBench](https://github.com/canyuchen/ClinicalBench) | Examine its matched cohorts, feature availability, task-specific baselines, reproducible configurations and saved-result rescoring. Ask which non-LLM reference can be defined from *our development data* without adding unavailable EHR features. | Its mortality and other EHR prediction tasks have different data and outcomes; it is not a direct baseline for a rare published case report. Do not confuse **ClinicalBench** with **ClinicBench**. |
| Difficulty with unsupported claims, unsafe assumptions, exact calculation or subgroup reporting | [Bedside Bench](https://github.com/doximity/bedside-bench) | Inspect its published failure categories, calculation requirements, rubric schema, duplicate checks and development/test split. Use these as probes of our solver or construction audit where applicable. | Its items are physician-authored and physician-adjudicated; we have no human reviewers. Its free-text grading can use an LLM judge, so its validity procedure cannot simply be copied. [Methods](https://technology.doximity.com/articles/bedside-bench-expanding-our-commitment-to-transparency-safety). |
| Need to report performance across forecast families without hiding weak categories | [MedHELM](https://medhelm.org/) | Study its clinical task taxonomy, per-task reporting and versioned benchmark releases. Check how grouping and model comparisons are described when tasks differ. | Broad medical task categories are not our labels; family weights and case clustering must remain declared in our own protocol. |
| Need to test whether the solver recognizes insufficient information | [MedQAbstain](https://github.com/disi-unibo-nlp/llm-medical-abstention) | Study its removed-answer and distractor controls as adversarial checks of answerability and overcommitment. Consider separate probes of our probability and uncertainty interface. | Its abstain answer differs from our `OTHER` option: `OTHER` means an observed event outside the slate, not missing evidence or refusal. Its medical safety interpretation also differs from forecasting the documented course. |

ClinicBench is the nearest conceptual neighbor discovered so far. Its own [project write-up](https://atlasdiscovery.bio/clinicbench) notes that plausible recommendations can diverge from actions actually taken. This is a central distinction for us: forecast the reported action under the stated cutoff, and analyze clinically plausible alternatives without relabeling them as events that occurred. It also reports that more tool calls did not track its model scores. That observation motivates measuring the *value* of each tool, not simply giving every solver a larger tool budget.

## Forecasting and benchmark engineering

| If we encounter… | Consult | Specific question to investigate | Transfer limit |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Temporal leakage, repeated forecast horizons or a future live benchmark | [ForecastBench](https://github.com/forecastingresearch/forecastbench) | Examine its dated question creation, forecast submission before resolution, outcome updates, probability scoring and separation of baseline versus tournament systems. [Protocol](https://www.forecastbench.org/about/). | It runs on future public events and time series. Our retrospective reports have already been published, so publication cutoffs and source exclusion still matter. |
| Need to compare tools, models, file delivery and failure recovery across scientific tasks | [LABBench2](https://github.com/EdisonScientific/labbench2) | Inspect its external runner interface, file/injection/retrieval modes, task tags, explicit failed-ID retries and result reports. | Its biology tasks and licenses differ. File delivery can change how much information reaches a solver; keep our input contract identical across strategies. |
| Need an outer evaluation runner and standard traces | [Inspect AI](https://github.com/UKGovernmentBEIS/inspect_ai) | Test adapter behavior, limits, logging and scoring after our deterministic core exists. [BixBench3](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.25286v1) is a concrete scientific use of Inspect with sandboxed execution and artifact scoring. | Inspect's ordinary task state includes the target; passing it directly to a solver would violate our hidden-label boundary. Preserve our raw transport record and case-cluster statistics. |
| Need reproducible experiment freezes, controls and failure accounting | [Anabasis](https://github.com/s-smits/anabasis) | Revisit its freeze/verification separation and negative-control machinery when comparisons become numerous. | Its autonomous solve workflow and JavaScript stack are broader than our first Python runner. Inspect actual isolation rather than inferring it from names. |
| Need programmatic orchestration, context inspection or recovery | [Prime Agent](https://github.com/PrimeIntellect-ai/prime-agent) and [RLM](https://github.com/alexzhang13/rlm) | Examine host request IDs, snapshots, child-call accounting and tool output handling when building the optional runtime. | A Python process is not a security sandbox. Freeze skills during evaluation and prevent provider calls outside the host broker. The [pinned source review](SCIENCE_HARNESS_INTELLIGENCE_2026-10-08.md#prime-agent-as-an-inspectable-programmatic-runtime) records the examined revision. |

## Specialized clinical tools and interactive environments

| If we encounter… | Consult | Specific question to investigate | Transfer limit |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Need structured medical-record actions or explicit invalid-action feedback | [MedAgentBench](https://github.com/stanfordmlgroup/MedAgentBench) | Inspect its virtual EHR tool contracts, task reset behavior and error states. | It tests interacting with a simulated EHR, not forecasting a fixed published report. Its server setup should not be imported merely to obtain a tool schema. |
| Need to test which facts a solver would request when evidence is incomplete | [AgentClinic](https://github.com/samuelschmidgall/agentclinic) | Study its measurement-request interface and synthetic patient/doctor interaction as a *separate* active-information experiment. | Its patient and measurement agents can generate data. Such generated observations cannot become factual continuations in our grounded benchmark. |
| Need a biomedical reference tool or evidence-backed computation | [Biomni](https://github.com/snap-stanford/Biomni), [AgentMD](https://github.com/ncbi-nlp/Clinical-Tool-Learning), [PaperQA](https://github.com/Future-House/paper-qa) | Compare narrow tool descriptions, required input checks, literature provenance and hidden secondary model calls. | Each tool needs a task-specific access and leakage audit. The case's own article stays excluded from retrieval. |
| Need to turn a fixed-prefix forecast into an interactive task with a defined environment | [Aviary](https://github.com/Future-House/aviary) and [LDP](https://github.com/Future-House/ldp) | Inspect how policies and tool environments are separated before designing a new interactive track. | A simulated response changes the task. It requires its own target, ground truth and validation protocol. |

## How to use this list

Before borrowing code or a result, write down the specific failure or feature being addressed. Check the current source revision and license, then compare the project's information boundary, output target, scorer, reviewer type, model/provider mix and human involvement with ours. Make a small development-set test of the borrowed mechanism against the existing baseline. A significant adopted change goes into the next design decision; an interesting paper alone does not change the protocol.

Prioritize **ClinicBench** when defining next-action contracts, **ForecastBench** when handling temporal validity, **Bedside Bench** when auditing unsupported claims and calculations, **LABBench2/Inspect** when implementing the runner, and **Prime Agent** when implementing programmatic execution. The other entries are issue-triggered references. This document is maintained with the project rather than implying that any external repository is continuously monitored.
